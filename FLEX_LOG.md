### Implications
- For the paper: no claim changes. One rounding: "the actual flexibility would be 3% higher" reproduces as 3.4%, still within tolerance.
- For Step 1, one fact the prompt does not account for. **The estimate's SF has no intercept.** Eq. (sf-target), `outputs.sf_target`, is SF = m_h (T_h − T) at the target's own threshold; b_h is not used (Table III). The prompt's W2 peak of 7–9 °C comes from the pilot curve with its intercept. Evaluated analytically on the estimate's curve (Fig. 11 split, no new data), the W2 peak moves to about 5–6 °C (T_in = 20–21 °C), and F on the coldest day stays at 0.78–0.81 of the peak. With the intercept, the coldest day is at 0.71–0.75 of the peak. So under W2, F(T) is a broad hump, not a sharp peak, and its location depends on b_h and T_in. These are derived numbers only; Step 1 measures them.
- For Step 1: since no observed day has SF ≥ 0.5, "P_up > P_down on almost all days" will hold by construction for the estimate, and W1 will be monotonic over the observed range.
- Decision for Alex: in the Expectations, I will record the prompt's 7–9 °C W2 peak for the actual (which has an intercept) and 5–6 °C for the estimate. Recommendation: accept, since the two curves differ in b_h by design, and logging one expectation for both would make one of them fail for a known reason.
